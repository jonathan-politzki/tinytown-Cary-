# Florida distressed office/retail and land sites: market study (market slice)

Prepared 2026-09-23. Every figure carries its source date. Brokerage vacancy figures differ by source because each firm defines its inventory differently. Compare figures from the same source over time, and don't mix sources.

Method note: the web-search budget for this session ran out partway through section 6 (who buys, and how they source deals). That section rests on fetched primary pages and on inference from the deal record. More vendor and competitor research is recommended before a build decision.

---

## Key takeaways

1. **"Bridge Industrial / Kurv" is one company.** Chicago-based Bridge Industrial renamed itself **Kurv Industrial** in March 2026. It kept the same leadership (co-founder and CEO Steve Poulos), structure and strategy. The stated reason was to stop being confused with similarly named firms. The main one is **Bridge Investment Group / Bridge Logistics Properties**, which is unrelated and now owned by Apollo, and which is also buying Florida industrial sites. Kurv's Miami office covers South Florida. Since 2012 it has done 24 South Florida transactions totaling more than 800 acres and has delivered or started more than 12M SF there.
2. **Kurv already does exactly what the friend describes: office-to-industrial in Miami-Dade.**
   - The former Ryder HQ bought for $42.1M in 2023 became Bridge Point Flagler Station (326K SF).
   - A 1972 office park at 7775 NW 48th St, Doral, bought for $45.2M in 2024, is becoming Bridge Point Doral 826 (269K SF, $56.7M construction loan in Feb 2026).
   - Its executives describe the strategy as reimagining "obsolete office parks and other underutilized properties."
   - Because the user is proven, a tool that finds the *next* Doral 826 is a real need, not a hypothetical one.
3. **Florida office is a two-tier market.** Trophy and Class A space in Brickell, West Palm Beach CBD and Westshore is tight, with record rents. **Older suburban Class B/C parks are where the distress is:** Boca Raton, Sunrise, Miramar, Maitland, Jacksonville suburbs, Tampa's I-75/Lakeview corridor. Statewide averages hide this.
4. **Retail distress in Florida is narrow.** Strip and grocery-anchored vacancy is roughly 3–4.5% (Q2 2026), near record lows. Distress sits in **regional malls and dead anchor boxes**:
   - Pembroke Lakes Mall ($260M CMBS loan, maturity default March 2025)
   - Miami International Mall (in special servicing since Feb 2024; appraisal down about 60%)
   - Washington Prime's 10-property Florida portfolio, which is for sale
   - Orlando Fashion Square
   - Seminole Towne Center
   Most of these sites are going to mixed-use or residential, not industrial.
5. **Capital-markets distress is high and still rising.** Nationally (Trepp/CREFC, 2026):
   - CMBS delinquency is about 7.5–7.9%, the highest since COVID.
   - Office delinquency was 11.7% in March 2026.
   - The special-servicing rate was 11.42% in August 2026, the highest since Feb 2013.
   - Office is about 50% of specially serviced balance.
   - About $875B of commercial mortgages mature in 2026, and about $652B in 2027.
6. **The best Florida conversion sites often come from corporate owner-occupiers shedding campuses, not from lender foreclosures.** Examples: Ryder, AT&T/Concentrix (Jacksonville), Mutual of America (Boca), Baptist Health (Boynton Beach). The signals for these are public: headquarters moves, lease terminations, relocation announcements, "largely vacant" campuses owned by operating companies. A useful tool should score **owner-user obsolescence**, not just loan default.
7. **Sourcing today is broker-led and competitive.** For example, 8000 Baymeadows drew "multiple bidders" in a brokered process. Developers say South Florida is "the most difficult place to find sellers that have real motivation." Data vendors cover pieces of the problem: Reonomy (Altus) predicts which owners will sell; Trepp and CRED iQ cover CMBS loans. No public source found a product that combines **Florida parcel, zoning and truck-access fit** with **distress and obsolescence signals** for industrial redevelopment. That looks like an opening, with caveats (section 6).

### Where the distress is concentrated
- **Suburban Class B office parks from the 1970s–1990s** in Boca Raton (Park of Commerce, Broken Sound, Yamato Rd), Sunrise, Miramar, Doral/Airport West, Maitland Center, Jacksonville Baymeadows/Southside, and Tampa Lakeview/I-75. Evidence:
  - Workspace Property Trust's South Florida buildings: portfolio occupancy fell from about 89% to about 75%.
  - The 1972 Doral office park Bridge bought.
  - The 1984 Mutual of America campus at Broken Sound.
  - The 1983 Transtech campus in Jacksonville.
  - The 1984 Lakeview office in Tampa.
- **Downtown Jacksonville** (28.7% CBD vacancy). Examples: the Independent Life Building foreclosure; the JEA tower sold for $1M for residential conversion (Aug 2025).
- **Broward 4- and 5-star vacancy of 18.8%** shows that even newer suburban stock is soft there.

## 2. Florida retail market

- **Fundamentals are very tight (Q2 2026):**
  - Miami-Dade 3.0%, +378,795 SF net absorption, $42.50 PSF
  - Broward 4.3%, **-102,436 SF** absorption, rents down 3.4% YoY
  - Palm Beach 4.0%
  - Tampa 3.9%
  - Orlando about 3.9%
  - Jacksonville 4.8% (Q4 2025)
- **Big boxes get backfilled quickly.** Former Big Lots and JoAnn boxes are being re-leased (Business Observer, Mar 2026). Examples: Wayfair took 94K SF at the Galleria, Fort Lauderdale; Floor & Decor took 55K SF in Kendall; Rural King moved into the former Sears at Lakeshore Mall (opened April 2026).
- **Distressed or redevelopment candidates are mostly enclosed malls:**
  - **Pembroke Lakes Mall** (Brookfield). $260M GSMS 2013-PEMB loan hit maturity default on 1 March 2025 and went to special servicing (KeyBank). Implied LTV is 163%.
  - **Miami International Mall** (Simon). In special servicing since Feb 2024. Forbearance runs to Feb 2027. Appraised at $143M in June 2025, against $391M at securitization.
  - **Washington Prime Group** is selling all 10 of its Florida properties (Apr 2025): Edison Mall, WestShore Plaza, Paddock Mall, Melbourne Square, Orange Park Mall, Gaitway Plaza, Royal Eagle Plaza, University Town Plaza, West Town Corners and others.
  - **Orlando Fashion Square**. 625K SF, listed for sale as a redevelopment site. The ground lease and building ownership are split. Its cinema closed in March 2026.
  - **Seminole Towne Center** (Sanford). Closed Jan 2025. Ardent bought about 76 acres for $17.5M for a roughly $700M Costco, apartments and mixed-use plan.
  - **Santa Rosa Mall** closed in Sept 2025.
  - **Southland Mall**: the former Sears and Kmart space is becoming residential.
  - Governor's Square and Oviedo Mall are struggling but open.
  - South Florida: more than 6 mall redevelopment plans, mostly adding apartments (TRD, July 2026).
- **Industrial takeaway:** retail-to-industrial has precedent nationally (CBRE, 2019: 23 projects, 7M SF of retail turned into 10M SF of industrial). But in Florida, housing and mixed-use currently outbid industrial for mall land, and industrial also faces rezoning and neighbor opposition. The best retail-to-industrial targets are **dead boxes or outparcels next to highways and existing industrial zoning**, not mall cores.

## 3. CMBS and bank-loan distress, and the maturity wall

### National context (Florida-specific CMBS rates are not published for free)
- Trepp CMBS delinquency:
  - 7.30% (Dec 2025): office 11.31%, retail 6.92%, lodging 6.61%
  - 7.85% (Aug 2026)
  - Office 11.71% (Mar 2026)
- Special servicing:
  - Office 16.73% (Mar 2026) and 17.11% (Jun 2026)
  - Overall 11.42% (Aug 2026), the highest since Feb 2013
- CREFC, May 2026:
  - Delinquency 7.55%; special servicing 10.86%
  - **About 50% of specially serviced balance is office**
  - The most common reasons for transfer are maturity and balloon defaults
  - 7 of 10 loan dispositions that month were office
- Office loans are about 42% of the delinquent balance ($47.4B total delinquent, Aug 2026).
- MBA (Feb 2026): **$875B (17%) of $5.0T commercial mortgages mature in 2026**, and $652B in 2027.
- More than 200 distressed office properties traded in 2025. Foreclosure and bankruptcy trades totaled more than $5B, and early 2026 was running ahead of that (TRD, Apr 2026).
- **Florida law helps buyers.** Chapter 714 (Florida's Uniform Commercial Real Estate Receivership Act, effective 1 July 2020) lets receivers sell property **free and clear of liens**, as in a bankruptcy §363 sale. Receivership sales are therefore a practical way to buy.
- Shumaker (2026): "Florida leads the nation in foreclosure activity," and Tampa Bay is at the center. Insurance costs and interest rates are hitting commercial net operating income.

**How the signals line up for a tool.** The public signals come in a predictable order:
1. A tenant leaves or terminates early (press, WARN notices, HQ relocation news).
2. Occupancy and debt-service coverage fall (CMBS reports).
3. The loan goes on the servicer watchlist, a cash sweep starts, or the rating is downgraded (KBRA, DBRS, S&P).
4. The loan is extended or modified.
5. The loan defaults at maturity and moves to special servicing.
6. An appraisal reduction is recorded.
7. A receiver is appointed or a lis pendens is filed (county clerk dockets).
8. A deed-in-lieu or foreclosure sale is recorded (county deed records).

Loan-level CMBS data is free on SEC EDGAR (Form 10-D Exhibit 99.1 and ABS-EE filings). Examples in 2026 include the 10-D filings for JPMBB 2014-C25 and SG 2016-C5. Bank-held loans, which make up most suburban office debt, show nothing until the county docket stage. That is the gap a tool would have to fill with proxies.

Foundry says it has done **11 office-to-industrial conversions nationally**, removing 25 office buildings (2.6M SF) and delivering 13 industrial buildings (4M SF) (Apr 2026). Nationally, about 23.3M SF of office was being converted or demolished as of June 2025, 10.5M SF of it by demolition.

**Retail and other users to industrial in Florida:**
- Bridge Logistics Properties (Apollo; *not* Kurv) paid $11M in 2025 for Orlando's 47-acre Frito-Lay site at 988 N John Young Pkwy. It plans 232K SF of spec warehouse, with a planning board hearing on 16 June 2026.
- Shuttered Walmart, Target, grocery and cinema boxes in Tampa have been converted.

**Why the land math works (Miami-Dade):**
- 88% of industrial land is developed, leaving about 30M SF, mostly remote.
- Developers see "5–6 years" of land left (Nov 2024).
- Industrial land costs about $3.5M per acre.
- Industrial sells at about $204 PSF and rents at $16–$20 NNN (Doral Class A $18–$20), 2026.
- Buying a 1970s office park at $45M for 16 acres ($2.8M per acre) is *at or below* raw land cost, and the zoning and access are already in place.

**Site criteria**, synthesized from the deals above, Foundry's stated criteria and Kurv's statements:
- **Acreage:** 10–30+ acres. The examples are 11.7, 16, 17, 20, 26, 30 and 47 acres. Flex product can work on about 5–10 acres.
- **Zoning:** prefer sites where industrial or warehouse is already allowed "by right." Foundry: "no zoning changes required … no approval constraints." Avoid sites next to homes.
- **Truck access:** directly on or within about 1 mile of a limited-access highway (SR-826, I-95, I-4, I-75, JTB). Room for 180' truck courts, rear-load or cross-dock layouts, and 32–40' clear height.
- **Location:** infill, close to population and labor; near ports and airports (MIA, Port Everglades, Port of Palm Beach, Opa-locka).
- **Basis:** low price per acre compared with industrial land, the ability to demolish, a manageable environmental situation (Gratigny was a former military site with wetlands), and a seller with a reason to sell. Motivated sellers include corporate owner-occupiers, lenders, receivers and estates.
- **Building profile:** low-rise suburban office (1–3 stories, surface parking, low floor-area ratio). High-rises almost never work.

## 5. Bridge Industrial / Kurv Industrial

- **Identity.** Founded in 2000 in Chicago by Steve Poulos. Renamed **Kurv Industrial** on 16–17 March 2026, with the same team and strategy, to avoid confusion with similarly named firms. Offices in Chicago, Los Angeles, Miami, New Jersey and Seattle; Dallas is an emerging market. Poulos: "The name Kurv reflects that mindset."
- **Scale** (kurvindustrial.com, 2026): more than 76M SF developed and more than 290 buildings. Miami region: 23 properties, about 11.4M SF, 25 buildings.
- **Florida portfolio** (Kurv site): Kurv Doral, Kurv Doral II (7705 NW 48th St), Kurv Davie 595 (677K SF, 3 buildings), Kurv Ave (Opa-locka airport), Kurv Commerce Center (Miami Gardens), Kurv Miami Station (Medley; the former Ryder HQ), Kurv Gratigny (4700 NW 135th St), Kurv Palmetto Lakes (Hialeah/Miami Gardens), Kurv Port Everglades (Dania Beach), and now Kurv Pompano.
- **Recent Florida deals:**
  - *Kurv Pompano* (East Pompano Industrial Center, 818.6K SF, 50% leased) bought for $219.7M from QuadReal on 8 April 2026. CBRE brokered it. It was South Florida's largest industrial deal of 2026.
  - *Kurv Gratigny* (409K SF on 26 acres, bought for $15.6M in 2022 through a ground-lease assumption) was fully leased to **Ryder System** in July 2026. That makes Ryder, whose old HQ Bridge demolished, the tenant of a new Bridge building.
  - *Bridge Point Doral 826* (above).
- **Capital:**
  - A US$789M JV with CPP Investments (announced 16 Jan 2025; CPP 95%, Kurv 5%) for acquisitions and development in supply-constrained US markets. It is funding the Pompano purchase.
  - A first CPP develop-to-core JV (2021) put money into Miami and LA.
  - Construction lenders include CDPQ and Associated Bank.
  - No 2025–26 closing of a numbered Kurv commingled fund was found (the search budget ran out).
- **Florida lead:** Kevin Carroll, Partner, Southeast Region. Kurv also appointed Gian Rodriguez as Market Officer, Miami (2026).
- **Not to be confused with:** Bridge Investment Group / **Bridge Logistics Properties** (Apollo bought Bridge Investment Group for about $1.5B in Sept 2025). It closed Logistics Value Fund II at about $1.4B in June 2026 and is buying Florida sites such as Orlando's Frito-Lay site and Gateway @ 429. It is a competitor for the same sites. Also unrelated: Kurv Investment Management, an ETF issuer.

## 6. Who buys distressed Florida office and retail, and how they source deals

**Buyer types (from the deals above):**
- **Industrial developers doing office-to-industrial:** Kurv (Bridge), Foundry (with Wheelock Street, Crow Holdings), Richland Capital, Butters, Bridge Logistics (Apollo). Nationally also Link, Dermody and Prologis on retail boxes (not verified here).
- **Multifamily and mixed-use converters:** Ram Real Estate, Ardent (Seminole Towne Center), Live Oak (JEA tower), Kolter (Live Local Act projects), and the mall owners' own redevelopment arms.
- **Opportunistic office buyers and lenders taking assets back:** Granite Point (Lincoln Place), Highland Ventures, and local buyers at around $40–$90 PSF.
- **Retail backfill users:** Costco, Publix (bought Fountains of Boynton for $78M and Aberdeen Square for $24M), Floor & Decor, Rural King, and entertainment operators (pickleball, Tilt, Round One).

**How deals are sourced today** (evidence plus inference):
1. **Brokered, often competitive sales.** CBRE National Partners sold Pompano. 8000 Baymeadows went through a "competitive sale process" with "multiple bidders." Edge is marketing Orlando Fashion Square. JLL leases the finished product. A developer's own brokerage arm also screens deals: Foundry says its brokers "screen opportunities" for its development team.
2. **Relationships with corporate owner-occupiers.** Ryder, Concentrix, Mutual of America and Baptist Health were all users selling surplus campuses, not lenders.
3. **Lender, special-servicer and receiver channels:** note sales, deeds-in-lieu, and Chapter 714 receiver sales.
4. **Data vendors (partial coverage):**
   - **CMBS loan performance:** Trepp and CRED iQ.
   - **Owner identification and sale prediction:** Reonomy (an Altus Group business) unmasks the owners behind LLCs and scores properties "most likely to transact in the next 12 months" using ownership tenure, debt and liens.
   - **Market data:** CoStar/LoopNet, Crexi, and county property appraisers.
   - **Free public data:** CMBS loan-level data on EDGAR.

**Is there an unmet need?**
- *For the tool:*
  - Developers say South Florida is "easily the most difficult place to find sellers that have real motivation" (Bisnow, Nov 2024).
  - The best sites (low-rise suburban campuses on highways, already zoned for industrial) are *not* the ones flagged by CMBS distress. They are owner-user or bank-financed properties that CMBS tools never see.
  - No vendor found combines (a) Florida parcel geometry, zoning, FAR and highway access, (b) building age and obsolescence, (c) owner type and tenure, and (d) public distress signals into an industrial-redevelopment score.
  - Kurv, Foundry and others have done several of these conversions each. Finding one extra Doral 826 early (about a $45M basis) would repay a tool's cost many times over.
- *Against the tool:*
  - Large developers already pay for CoStar, Trepp or Reonomy and rely on broker networks. They win many deals through relationships.
  - The number of good sites is small, perhaps dozens statewide.
  - Most FL retail is healthy, so retail leads will mostly be malls, which lean toward residential.
  - The strongest distress data for bank loans only becomes public late, at the lis-pendens stage.
- *Net:* the need is real but narrow. The value is **early, off-market identification of industrial-suitable suburban office and retail parcels in Florida**, not a general "distress finder."
